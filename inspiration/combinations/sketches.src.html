A prefix code assigns each symbol a codeword so that no codeword begins another. It rests on the idea of surprise, measured in bits.

Kraft's inequality says such a code exists exactly when the sum of 2−ℓ over the codeword lengths ℓ is at most one.

Huffman's construction meets it with equality. See also Shannon–Fano coding.

P · One grammar of states

Every control speaks the same few states, so nothing needs explaining: light crosses what you touch; corner brackets mark where the keyboard is (Tab through these); solid is chosen; a bar is what's open; gold is Iso's; faint is unavailable.

Q · Glyphs with a habit

One drawing rule for every icon, from crystal habits: strokes at 0°, 45°, 90° (60° for the hexagonal ones), 1.5 px, square ends, the note's chamfer wherever there's a corner. Shown at 16 and 32 px: whole multiples, so they stay sharp. With the new kinds (artifact, data, document, misc), and below, every kind as an Atlas node: files in the bundle as full nodes, links out of it as small ones beside their note.

R · Questions and problems, on the way

Second try, calmer. A question is a specimen label: catalogue number, the question in your words, the notes it's about in corner brackets, and one mark that fills as it's answered (hollow rose ◇, then malachite ◆ with the answer's first line). A problem is a crafting screen: the notes it uses as slots, and a progress bar of its steps. On the Atlas they stay ordinary boxes; the mark, or the bar along the foot, is the only difference.

U · A note's label, in place of "details"

Always there, one strip under the title, no field labels. The description is the italic line under the title, written in place (a faint rule holds its place while it's empty). The kind is three slots, so a question is one press away. The path is where the note lives: press a folder or the name to move or rename it. Tags are bracketed parts. A question gains what it's about and a mark to answer it; a problem, what it uses (a problem of several steps is a chain of problems). History runs down the margin (T). Deleting moves to the pane's menu, away from the name.
