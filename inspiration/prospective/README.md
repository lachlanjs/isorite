# Prospective themes

Themes that could join the machine, the mineral and growth. Each folder has a few freely licensed images from
Wikimedia Commons (see its `SOURCES.md`) where I could find good ones. The lists below name what else to look at. Search
terms are given so you can find more yourself; I haven't checked every result.

Ranked by how well I think each fits. That ranking is a guess.

## 1. Geological survey maps (`geological-survey-maps/`)

Where minerals and maps meet: the Atlas *is* a map of what lies under the surface. Geologic maps colour regions by
rock unit, draw faults and veins as lines with ticks, and carry a dense, beautiful legend; topographic maps add contour
lines.

- What it could give: region fills by folder (a unit colour, not an outline), the legend as a real panel, hatching for
  question and problem notes, fault-line symbols for broken links.
- Look at: USGS geologic quadrangle maps; William Smith's 1815 map of England and Wales; BGS 1:50 000 sheets; Swiss
  Swisstopo relief maps.
- Search: "William Smith geological map 1815", "USGS geologic quadrangle", "Swisstopo relief shading".

## 2. Thin sections under polarised light (`thin-sections/`)

A mineral sliced thin enough to see through, under crossed polarisers: grey rock turns into stained glass, and the
colours shift as the stage turns. It is lustre made into a system: colour comes from structure and angle, not paint.

- What it could give: the hover in `combinations/` with a reason behind it (colour by angle); a "polarised" view of
  the Atlas where notes light up by their kind; a reference for the rose and malachite tokens.
- Search: "thin section cross polarized light", "Michel-Lévy chart" (the chart of interference colours is itself a
  beautiful object).

## 3. Kintsugi (`kintsugi/`)

Broken pottery mended with gold lacquer, so the repair is the most beautiful part. Gold already means Iso's work; this
gives it a meaning: Iso's words are the gold seam in your notes, visible, honest about where they are.

- What it could give: how provenance looks (Iso's text in a note as a thin gold seam, not a coloured highlight); how a
  fixed broken link looks.
- Gray area: it's a well-worn metaphor in design writing. It works as a rule for one thing (provenance), not as a theme.
- Search: "kintsugi bowl", "kintsugi detail".

## 4. Vector displays and instruments (`vector-displays/`)

Vectrex, Tektronix storage tubes, oscilloscopes: lines drawn by a beam, glowing, with afterglow. Close to Alien:
Isolation, but about drawing, not text.

- What it could give: how the Atlas's lines draw and fade (a beam's afterglow, not a CSS fade); Lissajous figures for
  a loading state.
- Search: "Vectrex screen", "Tektronix 4014", "oscilloscope Lissajous", "Battlezone arcade vector".

## 5. Specimen labels and Isotype (`isotype-and-specimen-labels/`)

Museum mineral labels (catalogue number, name, locality, a hand-written line), specimen drawers, and Otto Neurath's
Isotype picture language (iso- again, by coincidence, and a system for making information legible).

- What it could give: what a note card looks like on the Atlas or in a list (number, name, kind, one line); a
  catalogue-number voice for note ids; a pictogram system for note kinds in place of ad hoc icons.
- Search: "mineral specimen label", "Natural History Museum mineral drawer", "Isotype Neurath chart".

## 6. Slime mould networks (`slime-mould-networks/`)

*Physarum polycephalum* grows a network between food sources that ends up close to efficient (famously, the Tokyo rail
experiment). Organic growth that solves a routing problem, which is what the Atlas's router does.

- What it could give: routes that look grown; an animation of the Atlas being laid out.
- Search: "Physarum Tokyo rail network", "Physarum simulation".

## 7. Diffraction patterns (`diffraction-patterns/`)

What a crystal looks like to X-rays or electrons: points on a lattice, symmetrical, dark ground. The structure of a
thing seen indirectly, which is close to what "understanding" is.

- What it could give: a mark or loading state; the empty Atlas's ground (the dot grid, made a lattice of a specific
  symmetry).
- Search: "Laue diffraction pattern", "electron diffraction pattern", "Rosalind Franklin Photo 51".

## Not downloaded, worth considering

- **Commutative diagrams** (category theory): the literal picture of isomorphism, arrows between objects that agree.
  Search "commutative diagram". It may fit how Iso shows that two notes say the same thing.
- **Technical and blueprint drawings**: the crafting screen's item drawing comes from here. Search "blueprint
  engineering drawing", "exploded view drawing".
- **Field notebooks** (geologists', naturalists'): hand-ruled, dated, sketched. A warmer counterweight to the machine.
  Search "geologist field notebook", "naturalist field journal".
