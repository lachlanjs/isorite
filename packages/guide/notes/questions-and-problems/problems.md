type: Concept
title: Problems
description: A problem is something to do, and you say when it is solved.
---

A problem is a note of its own, `type: Problem`, that uses the notes it names in its label: an exercise to try, a calculation to do, a thing to build. Like [a question](/questions-and-problems/questions.md "uses"), it stands beside those notes on the Atlas.

You mark it solved, with the mark at the end of its label (◇ unsolved, ◆ solved), which adds `solved:` and the date. Nothing checks it but you. Write your solution in the problem's note, under a heading, if you want it kept.

A problem has no steps inside it. One that takes several is several problems, each linking to the one before it as "requires" ([Write a link](/notes/links-and-their-kinds.md "see also") says how), in a folder of their own if they belong together: the Atlas then draws the chain, and each is solved in turn.

Given a problem, [Iso](/iso/iso.md "see also") checks your working rather than solving it: it says where a step goes wrong, and leaves the next step to you.

The things to do in this guide are problems, and [Start here](/start-here.md "see also") holds the first.
