A cylinder can be unrolled onto a plane without stretching. What does the Theorema Egregium say about its $K$?

## Solution

Unrolling keeps lengths, so it keeps $E$, $F$ and $G$.

By the Theorema Egregium $K$ depends on them alone, so the cylinder has the plane's $K = 0$.

Check: its principal curvatures are $1/r$ and $0$, whose product is $0$.
