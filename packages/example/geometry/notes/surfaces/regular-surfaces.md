---
type: Concept
title: Regular surfaces
description: A subset of ℝ³ covered by smooth charts whose differentials are one-to-one.
---

A subset $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is a **regular surface** if every point has a neighbourhood $V$ and a smooth map $\mathbf{x}: U \to V \cap S$ from an open set $U \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ such that

- $\mathbf{x}$ is a homeomorphism onto $V \cap S$, and
- the differential $d\mathbf{x}_q$ is one-to-one at every $q \in U$.

The second condition says that $\mathbf{x}_u$ and $\mathbf{x}_v$ are independent, so at every point there is a tangent plane $T_pS$ spanned by them, and everything that follows is measured in it.

The sphere, the torus and the graph of any smooth function are regular surfaces. A cone is not, at its vertex.
